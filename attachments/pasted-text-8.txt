KOSH — PATTERN FLOW INTELLIGENCE OVERHAUL + TRADING TERMINAL PINE SCRIPT EDITOR

Implement two major enhancements in Kosh:

1. A comprehensive improvement to the existing Pattern Flow Intelligence feature, focusing primarily on the quality, clarity, analytical depth and usefulness of its written insights.
2. A new Pine Script Editor and Indicator Execution Environment inside the existing Market Terminal, designed around the workflow and capabilities familiar to TradingView users.

These are enhancements to the existing Kosh application, not standalone prototypes.

Before making changes, inspect the current repository, existing Pattern Flow calculations, data structures, AI generation pipeline, Market Terminal architecture, chart engine, indicator system, persistence and existing tests.

Preserve all working functionality.

---

PART A — PATTERN FLOW INTELLIGENCE

1. Primary objective

The existing Pattern Flow Intelligence page has an acceptable visual foundation, but its written explanations and presentation of historical statistics are not sufficiently clear or insightful.

The objective is to make the page understandable to someone who opens it and immediately wants to know:

- What is happening?
- What has happened historically in similar situations?
- How frequently has it happened?
- What happened afterwards?
- How reliable or consistent were those historical outcomes?
- What does the current evidence suggest?

Do not simply add more numbers.

Every number displayed must contribute to an understandable insight, comparison or decision.

The page should be analytical, informative and concise—not cluttered with technical terminology or unexplained statistics.

2. Audit the existing implementation first

Inspect:

- All Pattern Flow Intelligence components and sections.
- Manual write-up generation.
- AI write-up generation.
- Historical pattern identification.
- Similarity and ranking calculations.
- Historical observation windows.
- Forward-performance calculations.
- Benchmark comparisons.
- Existing charts and tables.
- Data availability and missing-observation handling.

Identify every metric currently calculated but not displayed or meaningfully explained.

Identify every statement that is vague, ambiguous, technically inaccurate or difficult to understand.

Do not invent new metrics before understanding the existing calculations.

Reuse existing data and calculations wherever possible. Extend the existing engine where necessary.

3. Rewrite the manual intelligence narrative

This is the highest-priority requirement.

The manually generated write-up must be substantially improved.

Replace vague expressions such as:

- "Pattern ranked"
- "Still going"
- "229 months"
- "Strong resemblance"
- "Historical trend continues"

with clear, contextual statements.

Every statement should communicate an actual finding.

Examples of the required writing style:

Historical coverage

"Historical coverage: 229 monthly observations, equivalent to approximately 19 years and 1 month."

Historical occurrences

"14 comparable historical occurrences were identified across the available data."

Forward performance

"10 of the 14 historical occurrences closed higher one month later, representing a 71.4% positive outcome rate."

Benchmark comparison

"9 of the 14 occurrences outperformed the benchmark over the following three months."

Interpretation

"Comparable historical setups showed a positive tendency over the following month. However, the relatively small sample and variation in outcomes mean this should not be interpreted as a reliable prediction."

These are examples of wording only. Actual values must come from Kosh's calculations.

Writing rules

- Always explain what the number represents.
- Always include the relevant unit and period.
- Convert large month counts into years and months.
- Use percentages where they improve understanding.
- Distinguish observations from interpretations.
- Distinguish historical tendencies from predictions.
- Avoid unexplained technical terms.
- Never describe a pattern as successful merely because its similarity score is high.
- Never describe a pattern as confirmed if the underlying conditions are not satisfied.
- Clearly distinguish insufficient data from a negative result.
- Do not use artificial precision.
- Do not repeat the same insight in multiple sections.

4. Historical coverage and time-period presentation

Support meaningful comparisons across:

- Daily
- Weekly
- Monthly
- Yearly

Where appropriate, also support rolling periods:

- 1 month
- 3 months
- 6 months
- 1 year
- 3 years
- 5 years
- 10 years

Do not confuse the observation frequency with the length of historical coverage.

For example:

229 monthly observations = approximately 19 years and 1 month.

Present the following separately:

1. Historical data coverage.
2. Number of valid observations.
3. Number of comparable historical occurrences.
4. Number of observations with available forward outcomes.
5. Latest available observation date.

When comparing periods, use consistent dates and avoid comparing incomplete periods as if they were complete.

5. Historical pattern comparison

Improve the way the current pattern is compared with previous occurrences.

For every identified comparable occurrence, calculate and retain the information needed to explain:

- Date of occurrence.
- Pattern type.
- Similarity score, where supported by the existing algorithm.
- Market conditions at the time.
- Subsequent price movement.
- Forward returns.
- Maximum favourable excursion.
- Maximum adverse excursion.
- Benchmark-relative performance.
- Whether the historical outcome is complete or still developing.

Present historical occurrences through an accessible timeline or comparison visualisation.

Allow users to understand how the current setup resembles previous situations.

Do not treat every occurrence as equally relevant if the existing similarity model provides a meaningful distinction.

Avoid look-ahead bias. Historical comparisons must use only information available at the relevant historical point.

6. Forward-performance analysis

This section must become substantially more useful.

Calculate outcomes over the available horizons:

- 1 day
- 3 days
- 5 days
- 10 days
- 1 month
- 3 months
- 6 months
- 1 year
- Longer horizons where sufficient data exists.

For each horizon, calculate where supported:

- Number of valid observations.
- Positive outcome percentage.
- Negative outcome percentage.
- Average return.
- Median return.
- Best outcome.
- Worst outcome.
- Dispersion or variability.
- Maximum favourable excursion.
- Maximum adverse excursion.
- Benchmark-relative return.
- Number of observations outperforming the benchmark.

Do not display metrics where the underlying sample is insufficient.

Distinguish price return from benchmark-relative return.

Do not mix completed outcomes with incomplete forward periods.

Presentation

Use a clear comparison table and appropriate charts.

The user should be able to identify:

- Which period historically performed best.
- Whether the result was consistent.
- Whether the average was distorted by a few exceptional outcomes.
- Whether the stock outperformed the benchmark.
- Whether adverse movements were significant.

Do not make a high hit rate appear reliable when the sample size is small.

7. Daily, weekly, monthly and yearly comparisons

Introduce a clear period-comparison interface.

Allow the user to compare the identified pattern and its historical outcomes across daily, weekly, monthly and yearly observations, wherever the data supports it.

Keep observation frequency and forward-return horizon conceptually separate.

For example, a monthly pattern can still have 1-month, 3-month and 6-month forward outcomes.

Do not imply that monthly observations automatically represent monthly forward returns.

Show the available sample count for every comparison.

8. Manual write-up structure

Reorganise the manually generated narrative into a concise hierarchy:

A. What is happening now?

Explain the current pattern, direction, important levels and status.

B. Historical context

Explain the amount of historical data, the number of comparable occurrences and the quality of the comparison.

C. What happened afterwards?

Summarise forward outcomes over relevant horizons.

D. Relative performance

Explain whether the stock historically outperformed or underperformed its benchmark.

E. Risk and consistency

Explain the variation, adverse movements and limitations of the historical sample.

F. Overall interpretation

Provide a short, balanced conclusion that explains what the available evidence indicates.

The manual narrative must be generated from actual Kosh data and calculations.

Do not hardcode example statements or sample statistics.

9. AI-generated intelligence

Improve the AI-generated write-up separately.

The AI version should have greater research and interpretation capabilities than the manual version.

Where reliable public information is available, allow it to incorporate:

- Relevant company announcements.
- Corporate actions.
- Earnings and financial results.
- Industry developments.
- Macroeconomic events.
- Relevant historical news.
- Sector and benchmark developments.
- Events associated with comparable historical patterns.

Use web research only when it adds material insight.

Every externally sourced factual claim must have a source reference and publication date.

Distinguish:

- Verified company information.
- Market data.
- Historical observations.
- AI interpretation.
- Speculation or uncertainty.

The AI must not invent news, financial metrics, historical occurrences or causal relationships.

AI visualisation

Where useful, generate additional analytical visualisations:

- Historical occurrence timeline.
- Forward-return distribution.
- Stock versus benchmark performance.
- Historical outcome comparison.
- Relevant event timeline.
- Current pattern versus historical examples.

Use existing Kosh chart and visualisation infrastructure.

Do not create decorative charts that do not explain something.

10. Manual and AI output consistency

Both versions must use the same underlying Kosh calculations and data definitions.

The AI version may add research and interpretation, but must not silently change the underlying statistics.

Show the date and time of the analysis and the data coverage used.

Make it easy to understand what is calculated by Kosh and what is interpreted by AI.

11. Numerical integrity and data utilisation

Use all relevant data already available to Kosh.

However, do not maximise the number of displayed metrics merely for the sake of using data.

Instead:

- Identify unused data that can generate useful insights.
- Add calculations only when they have an analytical purpose.
- Use consistent return definitions.
- Handle missing observations correctly.
- Handle corporate actions and adjusted prices consistently.
- Avoid duplicate historical occurrences.
- Avoid look-ahead bias.
- Avoid survivorship bias where the available universe permits.
- Show sample sizes and data limitations.

Do not present an unavailable metric as zero.

Do not treat missing data as a negative outcome.

Do not extrapolate incomplete forward periods.

12. Visual and UX improvements

The existing design is acceptable as a foundation.

Improve the page where it materially improves comprehension.

Prioritise:

- Better information hierarchy.
- Clear section headings.
- Short explanatory descriptions.
- Readable comparison tables.
- Consistent date and number formatting.
- Tooltips for technical metrics.
- Expandable historical detail.
- Appropriate charts.
- Responsive layout.

Avoid unnecessary cards, oversized headings, decorative elements and duplicated information.

PART B — PINE SCRIPT EDITOR IN MARKET TERMINAL

13. Objective

Add a full-featured Pine Script editing and indicator workflow inside Kosh's existing Market Terminal.

The intended experience should be familiar to TradingView Pine Editor users.

Users should be able to:

1. Open the Pine Editor.
2. Create a new script.
3. Paste an existing Pine Script.
4. Select the intended Pine language version.
5. Edit the script.
6. Run or add it to the chart.
7. View the indicator on the existing Kosh chart.
8. Save the script.
9. Reopen and modify it later.
10. Manage saved custom indicators.

This must integrate with the existing Market Terminal and its chart engine.

Do not create a separate charting application.

14. Critical feasibility requirement: Pine Script compatibility

Pine Script is a proprietary language and runtime.

Do not assume that creating a code editor automatically makes Kosh capable of executing Pine Script.

Do not claim full Pine Script v5 or v6 compatibility unless the actual language and execution semantics are implemented and tested.

Before implementation, investigate whether a legitimate licensed or otherwise permitted execution solution exists.

If not, design a Kosh-owned Pine-compatible parser and execution engine.

The architecture must support incremental expansion of language compatibility.

Do not silently approximate unsupported functions or return fabricated indicator values.

For every unsupported function or language feature, return a clear compilation or execution error identifying the affected line and feature.

Maintain an explicit compatibility matrix for v5 and v6.

15. Pine Editor interface

Create a professional editor panel integrated into the Market Terminal.

The interface should include:

- Code editor.
- Script tabs.
- New script.
- Open saved script.
- Save.
- Save as.
- Rename.
- Delete.
- Pine version selector.
- Add to chart / Run.
- Remove from chart.
- Compile status.
- Error and warning panel.
- Editor settings.
- Keyboard shortcuts.
- Search and replace.
- Line numbers.
- Syntax highlighting.
- Code folding.
- Indentation.
- Bracket matching.
- Undo and redo.
- Autocomplete where supported.

Use a mature code editor component such as Monaco Editor if it fits the existing architecture.

Provide Pine-oriented syntax highlighting, completion and diagnostics.

Do not implement these as decorative controls. Every visible action must work.

16. Pine language version support

Provide a version selector for Pine Script v5 and v6.

The version must be derived from the script's "//@version=" directive when present.

Allow the user to select a version for new scripts.

Do not silently change the version directive in an imported script.

If the script requests an unsupported language version, explain this clearly.

Maintain separate version-specific language rules where required.

17. Execution engine

Design a real script execution engine.

The engine must be able to process the Pine language features required by the supported compatibility matrix.

At minimum, evaluate the feasibility and architecture for:

- Indicator declarations.
- Input declarations.
- Variables and reassignment.
- Historical series references.
- Conditional statements.
- Loops and functions.
- Built-in mathematical functions.
- Moving averages.
- RSI.
- MACD.
- ATR.
- Bollinger Bands.
- Highest and lowest.
- Crossover and crossunder.
- Plotting.
- Shapes and labels.
- Horizontal lines.
- Fill and background colouring.
- Bar colouring.
- Alert conditions.
- Multiple plotted series.
- Historical bar-by-bar evaluation.

Also investigate more advanced features:

- "request.security()".
- Multiple timeframes.
- Custom functions.
- Arrays.
- Tables.
- User-defined types.
- Persistent variables.
- Strategy declarations.
- Strategy orders and backtesting.
- Bar states and real-time execution.
- Drawing objects.

Do not claim support for any feature until its semantics have been implemented and validated.

18. Integration with Kosh chart engine

Use the existing Market Terminal chart and its current data pipeline.

Do not introduce a duplicate OHLCV provider.

The execution engine should receive the chart's available OHLCV series and relevant timeframe context.

It should return structured output that the chart can render.

Support, where implemented:

- Multiple plots.
- Overlay indicators.
- Separate indicator panes.
- Line and histogram styles.
- Colours and transparency.
- Shapes and labels.
- Horizontal levels.
- Background and bar colouring.
- Configurable indicator inputs.
- Recalculation when the symbol or timeframe changes.

Preserve the user's existing drawings, chart settings, indicators and layout.

Adding a custom indicator must not overwrite existing chart objects.

When the chart symbol or timeframe changes, the script should recalculate against the new series using the same script definition.

Do not use fake or synthetic market data for actual chart execution.

19. Indicator management

Create a Custom Scripts section within the existing Indicators area.

Users should be able to:

- Save scripts.
- Rename scripts.
- Duplicate scripts.
- Delete scripts.
- Edit saved scripts.
- Add saved scripts to the chart.
- Remove scripts from the chart.
- Keep multiple custom indicators active simultaneously.
- Configure indicator inputs.
- Reopen scripts without losing their code.

Persist scripts using Kosh's existing persistence architecture where appropriate.

Use stable script IDs and preserve version directives.

A script saved to the library must remain available after refresh and relaunch.

Distinguish saved scripts from scripts currently applied to the chart.

20. Error handling and execution safety

The editor must clearly display:

- Syntax errors.
- Unsupported functions.
- Type errors.
- Runtime errors.
- Resource-limit errors.
- Invalid input declarations.
- Incompatible language versions.

Errors should identify the relevant line and explain the issue in understandable language.

Never silently produce an incorrect indicator.

The execution environment must be isolated from arbitrary access to the browser, server, filesystem, network and user credentials.

Apply execution limits for:

- Maximum script execution time.
- Maximum historical bars.
- Maximum drawing objects.
- Maximum array size.
- Maximum loops or computation.
- Maximum number of active scripts.

Prevent one expensive script from freezing the entire Market Terminal.

21. Primary user script validation

The user will provide a frequently used Pine Script.

Use that script as a primary compatibility and regression test.

Preserve its original code exactly.

First:

1. Inspect the version directive.
2. Identify every function and language feature it uses.
3. Produce a compatibility report.
4. Identify unsupported features.
5. Implement the required features without changing the script's intended logic.
6. Run it against the same symbol, timeframe and historical bars used in TradingView where equivalent data is available.
7. Compare its plotted values and signals with TradingView.

Do not claim exact equivalence merely because the script compiles.

Validate:

- Indicator values.
- Signal timing.
- Historical values.
- Real-time/forming-bar behaviour.
- Timeframe changes.
- Input changes.
- Repainting behaviour.
- Missing-data handling.

If a TradingView comparison cannot be automated, provide a clear manual validation procedure.

The primary indicator must not be considered complete until its calculations and signals have been compared.

22. Implementation phases

Implement this in phases, but design the architecture for the complete workflow.

Phase 1 — Editor and persistence

- Editor interface.
- Version selection.
- Script tabs.
- Save/open/rename/delete.
- Syntax highlighting.
- Basic diagnostics.

Phase 2 — Core Pine execution

- Parser and interpreter.
- Series evaluation.
- Common indicators and mathematical functions.
- Plotting and overlay support.
- Chart integration.
- Explicit compatibility matrix.

Phase 3 — Advanced Pine features

- Multi-timeframe data.
- Drawing objects.
- Arrays and advanced language features.
- Alerts.
- More complex script behaviour.

Phase 4 — Primary indicator validation

- Use the user's supplied script.
- Close compatibility gaps.
- Compare calculations and signals against TradingView.

Do not represent Phase 1 as a fully functional Pine Editor.

Do not represent partial Pine compatibility as universal v5/v6 support.

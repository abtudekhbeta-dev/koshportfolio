KOSH — ADVANCED LIQUIDITY SWEEP & FAILED BREAKOUT SCREENER

Complete product, technical, detection-engine and implementation specification

0. Project objective

Build a specialised technical-analysis screening system inside the existing Kosh application.

The feature must identify stocks exhibiting two related but distinct price-action patterns:

1. Liquidity Sweep & Reclaim: Price penetrates a significant historical swing high or low, fails to sustain the breakout/breakdown, and reclaims the original level.
2. Failed Breakdown / Breakout Reversal: Price breaks an important support or resistance area, demonstrates rejection or indecision, and subsequently reverses through that area.

Both patterns must support bullish and bearish setups.

The screeners must work across all supported NSE and BSE stocks and provide selectable timeframes:

- Monthly
- Weekly
- Daily
- 4-hour
- 1-hour
- 15-minute

The system should identify strong setups without excluding potentially interesting borderline setups.

The objective is not to produce the smallest possible list. It is to identify genuine opportunities, distinguish their quality, and allow the user to decide which deserve attention.

This must be implemented as a functional feature inside Kosh, not as a standalone demo, static HTML prototype or disconnected screening engine.

PART 3 — SHARED SWING DETECTION ENGINE

This is one of the most important components.

Do not detect every local high or low as a meaningful liquidity level.

3.1 Individual swing lows

A swing low is a candle whose low is lower than the lows of the specified surrounding candles.

Use configurable left/right pivot confirmation.

Initial defaults:

- Left candles: 5.
- Right candles: 3.
- Minimum swing age: 8 candles.
- Maximum lookback: 100 candles.

These are starting values, not hard-coded universal rules.

A swing low must not become available to the confirmed detection engine until the required right-side candles have closed.

This prevents look-ahead bias.

3.2 Individual swing highs

Apply the inverse logic.

A swing high must be higher than the highs of the specified surrounding candles.

3.3 Equal lows and equal highs

Identify repeated swing levels.

For equal lows:

- Identify two or more separate swing lows.
- Compare their price levels.
- Allow a configurable tolerance.
- Group them into a liquidity zone.

For equal highs, use the inverse.

Use percentage and ATR-normalised tolerance.

Initial suggested tolerance:

- 0.15% to 0.35%, subject to ATR and price behaviour.

Do not merge unrelated levels merely because they are close.

Record:

- Number of touches.
- First touch date.
- Latest touch date.
- Average level.
- Zone boundaries.
- Age of the level.
- Whether it has already been swept.

3.4 Swing significance

Not all pivots deserve equal importance.

Assess:

- Swing age.
- Price movement away from the swing.
- Relative distance to surrounding swings.
- Number of retests.
- Whether it is an isolated pivot or repeated level.
- ATR-normalised prominence.

Use this information for quality ranking.

Do not automatically discard a relatively recent swing if it is otherwise structurally meaningful.

3.5 Swing-level history

Maintain an internal record of detected swing levels.

A level should have:

- Unique identity.
- Price or zone.
- Creation date.
- Confirmation date.
- Level type.
- Touch count.
- Previous sweep history.
- Current status.

Do not repeatedly recreate the same level on every scan.

PART 4 — SCREENER 1: LIQUIDITY SWEEP & RECLAIM

4.1 Bullish liquidity sweep

The basic pattern:

1. A meaningful historical swing low exists.
2. Price has moved away from that low.
3. Price revisits the area.
4. Price penetrates below the swing low or equal-low zone.
5. Price subsequently reclaims the level.

The penetration may happen through:

- An intraperiod wick.
- A candle close below the level.
- A deeper intraperiod sweep followed by a close above the level.

The scanner must distinguish these variations.

A simple touch of support without penetration is not automatically a liquidity sweep.

4.2 Bearish liquidity sweep

Apply the inverse:

1. Meaningful historical swing high exists.
2. Price moves away.
3. Price revisits the level.
4. Price penetrates above it.
5. Price subsequently reclaims the level from below by closing back under it.

4.3 Sweep-depth calculation

Bullish:

Sweep Depth % = (Swing Low − Sweep Low) / Swing Low × 100

Sweep Depth ATR = (Swing Low − Sweep Low) / ATR

Bearish:

Sweep Depth % = (Sweep High − Swing High) / Swing High × 100

Sweep Depth ATR = (Sweep High − Swing High) / ATR

Store both measurements.

Initial configurable defaults:

- Minimum sweep depth: 0.05 ATR.
- Maximum sweep depth: 1.5 ATR.

Do not apply these as rigid exclusion thresholds to all stocks and timeframes.

Use a tolerance band around the minimum and maximum.

For example:

- Clearly inside the range: normal classification.
- Slightly below the minimum: possible weak sweep, retain as Potential where other conditions support it.
- Slightly above the maximum: inspect whether the move is a structural breakdown rather than a sweep.

Do not classify a massive breakdown as a strong liquidity sweep merely because price eventually bounces.

4.4 Reclaim windows

Support the following variations.

Type| Bullish sequence
Same-candle reclaim| Price sweeps and closes above the old low in the same candle
Immediate reclaim| One candle sweeps below, the next closes above
Two-candle reclaim| Sweep, indecision/doji, bullish reclaim
Three-candle reclaim| Sweep, consolidation/rejection, bullish reclaim

Default maximum reclaim window: 3 completed candles.

Allow users to adjust the reclaim window.

The bearish version is the exact inverse.

Record:

- Sweep candle.
- Reclaim candle.
- Number of candles taken.
- Reclaim close.
- Original swing level.
- Penetration extreme.
- Reclaim distance.

4.5 Important distinction: developing sweeps

A candle currently trading below a historical swing low but not yet closed must not be classified as a confirmed sweep-and-reclaim.

It can appear as a Developing candidate.

If price moves back above the level before candle close, update the developing status accordingly.

At candle close:

- Re-evaluate the complete candle.
- Promote or remove the developing signal as appropriate.

Never present intrabar conditions as completed-candle confirmation.

PART 5 — SCREENER 2: FAILED BREAKDOWN / BREAKOUT REVERSAL

This screener must be broader than the precise liquidity-sweep screener.

It should detect rejection around meaningful support/resistance zones even when the price action does not meet the exact swing-level sweep definition.

5.1 Bullish failed breakdown

1. Identify historically meaningful support.
2. Price breaks below support.
3. Price fails to sustain downside continuation.
4. Rejection, indecision or consolidation develops.
5. Price reclaims the support area.
6. Optional subsequent price confirmation occurs.

5.2 Bearish failed breakout

1. Identify meaningful resistance.
2. Price breaks above resistance.
3. Price fails to sustain the breakout.
4. Rejection or indecision develops.
5. Price returns below resistance.
6. Optional subsequent downside confirmation occurs.

5.3 Support/resistance zones

Do not require an exact single-price level in this screener.

Construct zones from:

- Historical swing clusters.
- Repeated highs/lows.
- Consolidation boundaries.
- Previously tested price areas.

Use ATR and percentage tolerances to define zone boundaries.

The zone must be sufficiently grounded in historical price action.

Do not create arbitrary support/resistance lines from every small candle.

5.4 Rejection sequences

Detect:

- Immediate reversal.
- Breakdown followed by bullish engulfing/recovery.
- Breakdown followed by doji and bullish candle.
- Multi-candle recovery.
- Bearish equivalents.

Candlestick formations are supporting evidence, not the sole basis of classification.

An evening-star-like sequence can be recognised where appropriate, but do not rely on the pattern name alone.

Use actual OHLC relationships.

5.5 Distinguish reclaim from confirmation

For bullish patterns:

- Reclaim: close back above the support zone.
- Stronger confirmation: subsequent break above the reclaim candle's high or nearby local swing high.

For bearish patterns:

- Reclaim: close back below resistance.
- Stronger confirmation: subsequent break below the reclaim candle's low or nearby local swing low.

Display these stages separately.

PART 6 — THE MOST IMPORTANT REQUIREMENT: TWO-TIER SIGNAL CLASSIFICATION

Do not build an excessively restrictive screener.

Do not build an excessively loose screener.

The user explicitly wants a balance between the two.

If 10 setups are clearly strong and 5 are borderline but interesting, the scanner should show all 15 with separate classifications.

It must not discard the five borderline setups.

It must also not mix them with the ten strong setups as though their quality were equal.

6.1 Core pattern validity versus supporting quality

Separate the engine into three independent assessments.

A. Core structural validity

Determines whether the price-action pattern exists.

For a confirmed bullish liquidity sweep:

- Valid historical swing low or equal-low zone.
- Actual penetration below the reference level.
- Subsequent completed-candle reclaim above the reference level.
- No invalidating data or structural condition.

The bearish version is the inverse.

For failed breakdowns/breakouts, use the equivalent support/resistance rejection and reclaim requirements.

B. Supporting characteristics

These affect ranking, not basic eligibility:

- Swing significance.
- Swing age.
- Equal-level touch count.
- Sweep depth.
- ATR-normalised sweep.
- Reclaim speed.
- Reclaim candle body.
- Close position.
- Wick characteristics.
- Relative volume.
- Trend context.
- Subsequent confirmation.
- Distance from reclaimed level.

A weak volume reading should not automatically eliminate an otherwise structurally valid pattern.

A relatively shallow but legitimate sweep should not be discarded merely because it is not ideal.

C. Hard invalidation conditions

These can prevent or terminate a signal:

- No genuine historical level.
- No actual penetration for a liquidity-sweep signal.
- No reclaim for a completed reclaim signal.
- Sustained continuation through the broken level.
- Reclaim failure.
- Invalid or stale market data.
- Unusable OHLC relationships.
- Signal expiry.

Keep these distinct from merely weak quality characteristics.

6.2 Signal tiers

Implement these categories:

<badge color="success">Strong match</badge>

Strong setup

- Core structural conditions satisfied.
- Supporting evidence is convincing.
- No major weakness.
- Reclaim is completed.

<badge color="warning">Potential</badge>

Potential setup

- The structural pattern is present or developing.
- One or more supporting characteristics are weaker.
- Or the reclaim is still developing.
- The setup remains worth monitoring.

<badge color="info">Developing</badge>

Developing

- Current candle has not closed.
- Intrabar conditions suggest a possible sweep/reclaim.
- Status may change before candle close.

<badge color="danger">Invalidated</badge>

Invalidated

- Price has failed the reclaimed level.
- The original pattern is no longer active.

Do not display invalidated signals in the default active-results view. Allow users to inspect them through history.

Important distinction

A Potential signal must not be a random stock that happens to be near support.

It must have a recognisable structural relationship to the pattern.

A stock with no valid swing level, no meaningful penetration and no reversal behaviour must not be added merely to increase the result count.

6.3 Avoid overly restrictive filters

Do not require every supporting condition to be satisfied.

Examples:

- Relative volume below average: reduce ranking, not automatic rejection.
- Reclaim takes three candles instead of one: reduce ranking, not automatic rejection.
- Reclaim candle has a moderate rather than exceptional body: reduce ranking.
- Equal-low cluster has only two touches rather than three: retain if otherwise valid.
- Swing age is slightly below the default: Potential if the structure is still meaningful.

Expose these characteristics in the results so users can judge them.

6.4 Avoid excessive false positives

Do not:

- Treat ordinary support bounces as liquidity sweeps.
- Treat every lower wick as a stop hunt.
- Treat every bullish engulfing candle as a failed breakdown.
- Treat a close below support as a reclaim.
- Treat a current unclosed candle as a confirmed signal.
- Repeatedly report the same swing event on every scan.
- Keep presenting invalidated setups as active.
- Use arbitrary score thresholds to hide structurally valid signals.

PART 7 — QUALITY RANKING

Implement a transparent 100-point quality score.

Initial proposed weighting:

Component| Weight
Swing significance and age| 20
Sweep quality and ATR penetration| 20
Reclaim strength| 20
Relative volume| 15
Candle structure| 15
Subsequent price confirmation| 10
Total| 100

For Screener 2, adapt the same framework to support/resistance significance and rejection quality.

The score is a ranking mechanism, not a probability of profit.

Do not describe an 85-point score as an 85% success probability.

Score interpretation

Suggested initial bands:

- 75–100: Strong.
- 55–74: Potential.
- Below 55: Do not automatically discard if the core structural pattern exists. Show only if the user enables a broader/low-quality filter, and clearly identify its weaknesses.

These bands are starting points and must be calibrated against historical samples.

A score must never override a hard invalidation condition.

7.1 Explain the score

When a user opens a result, show:

- Swing quality.
- Sweep depth.
- Reclaim quality.
- Volume.
- Candle structure.
- Confirmation.
- Weaknesses or missing evidence.

The user should understand why a stock is Strong or Potential.

PART 10 — SIGNAL LIFECYCLE AND DUPLICATE PREVENTION

This is essential for reliable results.

Each signal must have a stable identity.

Use a combination of:

- Instrument identity.
- Exchange.
- Timeframe.
- Pattern type.
- Direction.
- Swing-level identity.
- Sweep event candle.

The same underlying event must not appear as a new independent signal on every refresh.

Lifecycle

1. Level identified.
2. Sweep developing.
3. Sweep confirmed.
4. Reclaim developing.
5. Reclaimed.
6. Reversal confirmed.
7. Invalidated or expired.

Not every event will pass through every state.

Store signal creation time and last update time.

Invalidation

Bullish:

- Reclaim level is subsequently lost according to the selected invalidation rule.

Bearish:

- Reclaim resistance is subsequently regained.

Distinguish an intrabar breach from a confirmed closing invalidation.

Expiry

Signals must expire when:

- The setup is no longer relevant.
- The level has been invalidated.
- A maximum age has been exceeded.
- A new independent structure replaces the original event.

Preserve history for review.

PART 11 — SCANNING AND PERFORMANCE ARCHITECTURE

Do not issue one independent browser request per stock.

The system must scale to the supported NSE/BSE universe.

Prefer:

- Server-side or batch scanning where infrastructure supports it.
- Shared OHLCV retrieval.
- Cached historical candles.
- Incremental updates.
- Deduplicated requests.
- Shared calculations between screeners.
- Scan results cached by timeframe, universe and scan configuration.
- Efficient pagination and sorting.

11.1 Completed candles

Confirmed signals should be recalculated when a candle closes.

Do not continuously rescan years of history on every page render.

For completed candles:

- Process new data incrementally.
- Preserve previously validated signals.
- Recalculate only when necessary.

11.2 Developing signals

Developing signals may update more frequently depending on source refresh availability.

Clearly distinguish:

- Actual live/intraday data.
- Delayed data.
- Cached data.
- Last completed candle.

Never imply a live scan if the underlying feed is delayed.

11.3 Failure handling

If a particular stock has insufficient data:

- Skip that instrument for that scan.
- Record the reason internally.
- Do not fabricate results.
- Do not fail the entire scan.

If one exchange or timeframe data source fails, continue scanning other supported instruments where possible.

Show an accurate coverage indicator.

PART 12 — HISTORICAL VALIDATION AND BACKTESTING

This must be included in the implementation, not postponed indefinitely.

The scanner should be validated using historical OHLCV already accessible through Kosh's existing data infrastructure.

No user-supplied CSV should be required for normal operation.

12.1 Historical signal replay

Run the detection engine through historical candles in chronological order.

Important:

- Do not use future candles to identify a swing before it was confirmed.
- Do not use future candles to confirm a reclaim.
- Developing and confirmed signals must be evaluated separately.
- Do not use today's constituent universe as though it were historically unchanged where point-in-time universe data is available.
- Account for corporate actions.
- Avoid counting repeated scans of the same setup as multiple signals.

12.2 Forward-performance analysis

For every valid signal, calculate:

- Forward return after 1 candle.
- Forward return after 3 candles.
- Forward return after 5 candles.
- Forward return after 8 candles.
- Maximum favourable excursion.
- Maximum adverse excursion.
- Invalidation frequency.
- Time to reach predefined movement thresholds.

Run bullish and bearish analysis separately.

Run each timeframe separately.

12.3 Avoid misleading performance claims

Do not claim that the pattern guarantees a sharp price move.

Report actual measured historical performance.

Show sample sizes.

If historical data is insufficient, state that clearly.

Do not optimise thresholds on one stock and present the resulting performance as generalisable.

Use separate calibration and validation samples where sufficient data exists.

12.4 Test both precision and coverage

Measure:

- Strong-match precision.
- Potential-setup precision.
- Overall pattern coverage.
- Missed valid setups in manually reviewed samples.
- False-positive rate.
- Duplicate rate.
- Signal stability under small parameter changes.

The objective is balanced detection, not maximising precision by discarding every borderline opportunity.

PART 17 — ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

The work is complete only when:

1. Both screeners work independently.
2. Both bullish and bearish versions work.
3. All supported NSE/BSE instruments are scanned where data permits.
4. Monthly, weekly, daily, 4H, 1H and 15m are supported where data exists.
5. Individual and repeated swing levels are supported.
6. Immediate and multi-candle reclaim patterns are detected.
7. Developing and confirmed signals are distinct.
8. Strong and Potential signals are separated.
9. Borderline structurally valid setups are not automatically discarded.
10. Ordinary bounces are not misclassified as liquidity sweeps.
11. Ordinary breakdowns are not misclassified as failed breakdowns.
12. Signals are not duplicated.
13. Invalidated signals are not shown as active.
14. No look-ahead bias is present.
15. Chart navigation and annotations work.
16. Historical validation produces genuine measured results.
17. Performance is tested across the actual supported stock universe.
18. Data limitations are handled transparently.
19. Existing Kosh features are not broken.

FINAL PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

Kosh must identify structurally meaningful liquidity sweeps and failed breakouts without becoming excessively restrictive.

Strong setups must remain easy to find.

Potential setups must not disappear merely because one supporting characteristic is weaker.

Developing setups must not be confused with confirmed setups.

The user must be able to inspect every signal on the actual chart and understand precisely why it was detected.

Build a reliable, explainable, balanced screening system — not a collection of arbitrary technical filters.

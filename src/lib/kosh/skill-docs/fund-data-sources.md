# Verified Data Sources Priority

Purpose: Ensure every equity analysis is based on the latest available, verified information, with primary sources preferred wherever possible.

## 1. Indian Stocks — NSE/BSE

Use this priority order:

### Tier 1 — Primary Sources

1. Company Investor Relations website
2. Latest Annual Report
3. Latest quarterly results
4. Investor presentations
5. Earnings-call transcripts
6. Company exchange announcements
7. BSE filings
8. NSE filings
9. SEBI/regulatory filings

These should be the foundation for:

* Revenue
* EBITDA
* PAT
* EPS
* Margins
* ROCE/ROIC
* Debt
* Cash flow
* Capex
* Management guidance
* Business segments
* Order book
* Capacity expansion
* Promoter ownership
* Related-party transactions
* Auditor observations
* Capital allocation

### Tier 2 — High-Quality Secondary Sources

Use when useful for historical or comparative data:

* Screener.in
* Trendlyne
* Other reputable financial-data providers

Useful for:

* Historical financial series
* Historical valuation
* Peer comparison
* Shareholding
* Consensus estimates
* Historical ratios
* Earnings trends

Important: Secondary-source numbers should be cross-checked against primary filings when material to the thesis.

### Tier 3 — Reputable Financial Media

Examples:

* Moneycontrol
* Economic Times
* Business Standard
* Reuters
* CNBC-TV18
* Mint
* Financial Express

Useful for:

* Recent developments
* Management comments
* Industry developments
* M&A
* Regulatory developments
* News flow

Material claims should be verified against company filings, transcripts, or regulator sources wherever possible.

## 3. Small and Mid-Cap Rule

Small and mid-cap companies require deeper verification.

Never rely on a single aggregator.

At minimum:

1. Open the latest annual report.
2. Open the latest quarterly result.
3. Review the latest investor presentation.
4. Review the latest available earnings-call transcript.
5. Check exchange/regulatory filings.
6. Cross-check important financial figures against another credible source.
7. Search for at least two recent independent credible analyst/media discussions when available.
8. Check promoter/shareholding information.
9. Check auditor comments and qualifications.
10. Check related-party transactions.
11. Check contingent liabilities.
12. Check cash-flow quality.

If information is unavailable:

* Explicitly state the data gap.
* Do not infer precision that the available evidence does not support.
* Reduce confidence appropriately.

## 4. Forward-Looking Data

Forward-looking information is mandatory for the multi-bagger assessment.

Look for:

### Management Guidance

Prefer:

1. Latest earnings-call transcript
2. Latest investor presentation
3. Latest annual report
4. Official company announcement

Extract:

* Revenue guidance
* EBITDA guidance
* EBITDA-margin guidance
* PAT guidance
* Volume guidance
* Capacity guidance
* Capex guidance
* New-store/unit guidance
* Order-book execution guidance
* Medium-term growth targets

Always record:

Source + date

Example:

Management guided for ~20% revenue growth in FY27 during the Q1 FY27 earnings call dated July 2026.

Do not present management guidance as fact.

Clearly label it as:

Management guidance

## 5. Guidance Credibility

Compare management's historical guidance with actual outcomes.

Preferably examine the previous 3–4 years.

Classify:

### High Credibility

* Frequently met guidance
* Frequently exceeded guidance
* Conservative guidance
* Transparent explanations for misses

### Medium Credibility

* Generally achieved targets
* Occasional misses
* Reasonable explanations

### Low Credibility

* Repeated misses
* Frequent target changes
* Aggressive promises without delivery
* Guidance withdrawn repeatedly
* Significant divergence between promised and actual performance

Guidance credibility should materially affect the Management & Guidance Credibility score.

## 6. Consensus Estimates

When credible Street consensus exists, obtain:

* Revenue estimate
* EBITDA estimate
* PAT/EPS estimate
* Expected EPS growth
* Forward P/E
* Forward EV/EBITDA
* PEG where available

Always state:

Source + date

If consensus coverage is weak or unavailable:

* Say so explicitly.
* Do not manufacture a consensus estimate.
* Rely more heavily on management guidance and independent fundamental assessment.
* Reduce confidence in valuation precision.

## 7. Current Market Data

For valuation calculations, use the latest available:

* Share price
* Market capitalization
* Enterprise value
* Shares outstanding
* Net debt/cash
* Latest EPS
* Forward EPS
* Consensus EPS where available

Always date market-sensitive information.

Do not use stale valuation figures when newer information is available.

## 8. Source Discipline

Every important numerical claim should have a source.

For example:

Revenue CAGR: 24% (FY22–FY26, company annual reports).

Forward P/E: 31× (consensus EPS, source/date).

Promoter holding: 54.2%, down from 57.1% (shareholding filings, latest quarter).

Avoid unnecessary citations for obvious analytical conclusions, but cite the underlying data.

## 9. Conflicting Data

When sources disagree:

1. Prefer primary company/regulatory filings.
2. Check the reporting period.
3. Check whether one source uses consolidated and another standalone figures.
4. Check accounting-period differences.
5. Explain material discrepancies.

Never silently choose whichever number supports the thesis.

## 10. Final Source Principle

The objective is not to collect the maximum number of sources.

The objective is:

Latest + verified + relevant + preferably primary.

Use sources to establish facts.

Use analytical judgment to interpret those facts.

Never reverse this order.

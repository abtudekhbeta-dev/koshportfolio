# Qualitative Multi-Bagger Catalyst Analysis

Evaluate Indian listed companies for qualitative multi-bagger potential over a 2–5 year horizon.

Qualitative catalysts are the primary decision layer. Financial metrics are a supporting health check and must not mechanically override a strong, well-evidenced qualitative thesis.

Read `references/catalyst-framework.md` before scoring factors, applying synergies, adjusting for business model, or issuing a verdict.

## When to Use

- User provides one or more Indian listed stocks (name or ticker)
- User asks for multi-bagger potential, qualitative catalyst analysis, inflection points, or a qualitative verdict
- User wants qualitative filtering after a quantitative screen
- User wants to compare shortlisted names on catalyst quality

## Research Before Judging

Research the latest available information before forming a verdict.

Source hierarchy (use the strongest available evidence):

1. **Primary** — exchange filings, company announcements, annual reports, quarterly results, investor presentations, earnings-call transcripts, official company site, official regulatory documents
2. **High-quality secondary** — reputable financial publications, established data providers, accessible broker research, industry publications
3. **Discovery only** — general articles, forums, social media, aggregators. These may surface leads. Do not treat them as confirmed company facts.

Never invent financial figures, order books, capacity plans, guidance, partnerships, timelines, or management statements. Distinguish confirmed facts from interpretation. Mark unavailable or non-applicable items explicitly.

Prefer measurable evidence — capacity, commissioning dates, utilization, order-book values, order-book/revenue, customers, launches, commercialization, guidance, capex, debt actions, promoter actions, partnerships, restructuring.

## Core Process

1. Identify the company, ticker, and exchange.
2. Research latest primary and high-quality sources.
3. Produce a concise **Financial Snapshot** against the reference thresholds.
4. Score all seven qualitative dimensions using the framework.
5. Identify high-impact catalyst synergies. Apply judgment — do not mechanically score co-occurrence.
6. Classify style when useful — Consistent Compounder / Turnaround-Inflection / Mixed.
7. Issue exactly one approved verdict with a short rationale.
8. If multiple stocks, analyse each individually in the same format, then optionally add a comparative takeaway.

## Financial Snapshot

Reference thresholds (supporting indicators, not hard pass/fail):

- Sales growth 3Y CAGR > 18%
- Profit growth 3Y CAGR > 35%
- Profit growth 5Y CAGR > 20%
- Latest ROCE > 20%
- Debt / Equity < 0.5
- Promoter holding > 50%
- TTM operating profit margin > 15% (above 12% may be acceptable when the qualitative setup is unusually strong)
- Recent 1Y sales growth > 12%

Interpret in context of cycles, newly commissioned capacity, acquisitions, demergers, exceptional base years, turnarounds, transformations, and financial/holding-company models.

Classify as exactly one of: **Financially Strong** / **Acceptable** / **Mixed** / **Weak**.

Report only the 2–5 most decision-relevant observations. Note missing data. Do not estimate missing metrics.

A company with weak current financials can still have a credible Turnaround-Inflection thesis if future-improvement evidence is strong. Excellent historical financials do not make a high-potential multi-bagger if future catalysts are weak.

## Qualitative Dimensions

Score every dimension as exactly one of: **Strong** / **Moderate** / **Weak** / **Not Present**. Give brief evidence.

1. Capacity & Expansion
2. Product / Business Mix
3. Order Book & Demand Visibility
4. Structural / Thematic Tailwinds
5. Management & Corporate Actions
6. Operating Leverage & Inflection
7. Market Positioning

Do not force a factor where it is genuinely irrelevant. For financials, holding companies, asset-light models, and commodities, adapt using the business-model rules in the framework.

Evaluate each important catalyst on evidence strength, business impact, timing/visibility, and incrementality. A theme or rumour is not a company-specific catalyst.

Prioritize catalysts that are credible, material, approaching, and measurable.

## High-Impact Synergies (Reference Patterns)

| Combination                                                  | Reference Strength |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------ |
| New Capacity + Clear Numerical Guidance                      | Very High          |
| Large Order Book + Structural Theme                          | Very High          |
| New High-Value Product + Operating Leverage                  | High               |
| Turnaround + Debt Reduction + Promoter Commitment            | High               |
| Product Mix Upgrade + Structural Theme + Guidance            | High               |
| Capacity Expansion + Product Refresh + Strategic Partnership | High               |
| Only one moderate factor                                     | Low–Medium         |

Do not award a combination unless the underlying evidence supports the relationship.

## Verdict Labels

Use exactly one:

- High Potential Multi-bagger
- Moderate to High Potential
- Moderate Potential
- Low / Speculative Potential
- Not Attractive on Qualitative Factors

Do not equate strong company, strong theme, large order book, high growth, or low valuation with a multi-bagger. The question is what can materially change earnings power, competitive position, or market perception over 2–5 years, and how credible that change is.

Do not present a multi-bagger outcome as a prediction or guarantee. Do not invent target prices or probability percentages unless the user explicitly asks.

## Multiple Stocks

Analyse every company individually with the same structure and comparable research depth. After the individual write-ups, optionally add:

**Comparative Takeaway**

Rank on catalyst strength, timing, earnings visibility, financial quality, execution evidence, and runway. Explain the most important difference between the top names. Do not replace individual analyses with a comparison table.

## Hard Rules

- Base every material claim on verified information.
- If evidence is weak, limited, or missing, say so.
- Never treat generic sector narratives as company-specific catalysts.
- Do not overweight low institutional ownership by itself.
- Do not assume announced projects complete or orders convert to revenue.
- Do not assume historical growth continues.
- Keep output decision-oriented. Do not dump every researched data point.
- When browser research is used, cite or link important current evidence. Prefer primary sources.
- The Financial Snapshot supports the thesis. Qualitative catalysts drive the conclusion.

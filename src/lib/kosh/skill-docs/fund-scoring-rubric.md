# Scoring Rubric — Multi-Bagger Lens

Score strictly.

A score of 8+ represents clear excellence that genuinely supports multi-fold return potential.

Do not award high scores merely because a company is popular, profitable, or a high-quality business.

The score must reflect future multi-bagger potential from the current valuation.

## 1. Business Quality & Expandable Moat — 20%

9–10

Strong and expandable moat.

Examples:

* Pricing power
* Network effects
* High switching costs
* Scale-driven cost advantage
* Powerful brand
* Regulatory moat
* Customer stickiness
* Structural operating leverage

The competitive advantage should become stronger or more valuable as the company scales.

7–8

Solid competitive position with some expansion potential.

5–6

Average business.

Limited durable advantage.

0–4

Weak/no moat, easy to disrupt, structurally declining or poor economics.

## 2. Growth Potential & Runway — 25%

Highest-weight category.

9–10

Clear multi-year runway with:

* Multiple growth levers
* High credible growth
* Underappreciated opportunity
* Margin expansion potential
* Large addressable market
* Strong reinvestment opportunities

7–8

Solid above-industry growth with visible drivers for at least 3 years.

5–6

Moderate/in-line growth.

0–4

Low growth, cyclical peak, structural decline, or limited runway.

## 3. Capital Allocation & Incremental Returns — 15%

9–10

Excellent capital allocation.

Characteristics:

* High ROCE reinvestment
* Strong incremental returns
* Disciplined acquisitions
* Productive capex
* Intelligent shareholder returns
* Rising incremental ROCE

7–8

Generally good allocation and healthy returns on new capital.

5–6

Average/mixed history.

0–4

Value destruction.

Examples:

* Poor M&A
* Low incremental ROCE
* Frequent dilution
* Unproductive capex
* Poor capital discipline

## 4. Management & Guidance Credibility — 15%

9–10

* High skin-in-the-game
* Strong alignment
* Transparent communication
* Consistent guidance delivery
* Clean governance
* Strong capital-allocation history

7–8

Competent and mostly reliable.

5–6

Average track record.

Occasional misses or governance concerns.

0–4

Examples:

* Repeated guidance misses
* High promoter pledging
* Related-party concerns
* Poor alignment
* Major governance issues
* Questionable disclosures

## 5. Valuation vs Quality of Growth — 15%

9–10

Attractive valuation relative to:

* Growth
* Duration
* Quality
* Reinvestment economics

Meaningful margin of safety even under conservative assumptions.

7–8

Reasonable valuation for the quality and growth.

5–6

Fairly valued.

Limited asymmetry.

0–4

Examples:

* Expensive
* Priced for perfection
* Aggressive growth already embedded
* Poor risk/reward

## 6. Risk Asymmetry / Downside Protection — 10%

9–10

* Strong balance sheet
* Low permanent-capital-loss risk
* Clear asymmetric upside
* Strong downside protection

7–8

Acceptable downside with attractive upside.

5–6

Balanced risk/reward.

0–4

Examples:

* High permanent impairment risk
* Excessive leverage
* Governance risk
* Fragile business economics
* Limited upside

## Overall Score

Calculate:

Business Quality × 20% + Growth × 25% + Capital Allocation × 15% + Management × 15% + Valuation × 15% + Risk Asymmetry × 10%

Round to one decimal place.

Example:

Business = 8.0
Growth = 9.0
Capital Allocation = 8.0
Management = 8.0
Valuation = 7.0
Risk = 8.0

Overall:

8.0×0.20 + 9.0×0.25 + 8.0×0.15 + 8.0×0.15 + 7.0×0.15 + 8.0×0.10
= 8.0

## Verdict Language

Use exactly one of these:

### High-Conviction Multi-Bagger Candidate

Requirements:

* Overall score ≥ 8.0
* Clear growth runway
* Good risk asymmetry

A high score alone is insufficient.

### Quality Compounder

Use when:

* Business quality is strong
* Growth is credible
* Long-term compounding is attractive

But the opportunity may be steadier/slower rather than an obvious multi-bagger at current valuation.

### Speculative Multi-Bagger

Use when:

* Upside can be very large
* But execution, governance, valuation, business, or disclosure risk is materially higher

### Fair Value Compounder

Use when:

* Business quality is good
* Growth is credible
* But current valuation leaves limited multi-bagger upside

### Limited Asymmetry

Use when:

* Upside exists
* But not enough relative to downside or execution risk

### Avoid

Use when:

* Business is weak
* Capital allocation is poor
* Governance is problematic
* Growth runway is inadequate
* Valuation is severely stretched
* Permanent capital-loss risk is high

## Important Scoring Discipline

Do not inflate scores.

A company should generally require strong evidence to receive:

* 8+ for Business Quality
* 8+ for Growth
* 8+ for Management
* 8+ for Valuation

Especially:

8+ overall is exceptional.

The score is not a popularity rating.

It is an assessment of:

Future multi-fold return potential from today's price.

## Multi-Bagger Override Principles

A company should not receive a High-Conviction Multi-Bagger Candidate rating merely because:

* Revenue is growing rapidly
* EPS is growing rapidly
* ROCE is high
* The business is excellent
* The stock is popular

The combination must work.

The ideal profile is:

High-quality business + Large and expanding runway + High incremental returns + Credible management + Clean governance + Strong cash generation + Reasonable valuation + Asymmetric upside

## Valuation Override

Even an exceptional company may receive:

Fair Value Compounder

or

Limited Asymmetry

if current valuation already prices in:

* Aggressive revenue growth
* Sustained high margins
* Large margin expansion
* Multiple expansion
* Near-perfect execution

The question is always:

How much future success is already reflected in today's price?

## Risk Override

If governance, leverage, accounting quality, promoter pledge, or other issues create a material probability of permanent capital loss, the overall assessment must reflect that risk.

Do not allow high growth to hide severe downside risk.

## Final Decision Rule

The final verdict must answer:

1. Can this company compound earnings for years?
2. Is the runway large enough?
3. Can new capital earn attractive returns?
4. Is management credible?
5. Is governance clean enough?
6. Is valuation reasonable relative to growth?
7. Is the upside meaningfully larger than the downside?

Only when the evidence supports these questions should the company qualify as a high-conviction multi-bagger candidate.

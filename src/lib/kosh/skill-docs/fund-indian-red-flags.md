# Indian Market Specific Red Flags — NSE/BSE

Apply this checklist to every Indian listed-company analysis.

Material red flags must be explicitly mentioned and must reduce the relevant Management, Financial Quality, Growth Quality, or Risk Asymmetry assessment.

Do not treat every minor issue as a thesis breaker.

Focus on issues capable of causing permanent capital loss or materially impairing the multi-bagger thesis.

## 1. Ownership & Promoter Alignment

Check:

* Promoter holding %
* Promoter holding trend
* Promoter buying/selling
* Promoter pledging %
* Change in pledging
* Insider transactions

Major red flags

* Promoter pledge above roughly 20–25%
* Rapidly increasing pledge
* Large unexplained promoter selling
* Persistent decline in promoter ownership
* Promoters selling while simultaneously communicating aggressive growth
* Complex ownership structures

Interpretation:

High or rising promoter pledging can materially increase downside risk.

Do not automatically treat every promoter sale as negative. Determine whether there is a credible explanation such as:

* Tax
* Estate planning
* ESOP obligations
* Debt repayment
* Regulatory requirement
* Strategic transaction

## 2. Related-Party Transactions

Check:

* Related-party sales
* Related-party purchases
* Loans to related entities
* Guarantees
* Advances
* Investments
* Property transactions
* Inter-company arrangements

Red flags include:

* Material transactions relative to company size
* Persistent related-party dependence
* Non-arm's-length pricing
* Loans/guarantees benefiting promoter-linked entities
* Complex transactions that obscure economic performance

## 3. Auditors

Check:

* Auditor changes
* Sudden resignation
* Qualifications
* Emphasis of matter
* Internal-control weaknesses
* Delayed filings
* Accounting disputes

Frequent unexplained auditor changes are a major governance warning.

## 4. Accounting Quality

Check:

* Reported PAT versus operating cash flow
* Revenue recognition
* Receivable growth
* Inventory growth
* Capitalized expenses
* Changes in accounting policies
* One-time gains
* Exceptional items
* Other income dependence

Red flag

Profit grows substantially faster than cash generation for several years without a convincing working-capital or business explanation.

## 5. Receivables

Track:

* Receivable days
* Receivables as % of revenue
* Growth versus sales

Red flags:

* Receivables growing materially faster than revenue
* Persistent deterioration
* Large overdue balances
* Customer concentration

## 6. Inventory

Track:

* Inventory days
* Inventory growth versus revenue
* Obsolescence risk

Red flags:

* Inventory accumulation without corresponding sales
* Sudden inventory build
* Falling inventory turnover
* Large write-offs

## 7. Dilution

Check:

* Preferential allotments
* Warrants
* Convertible securities
* QIPs
* Rights issues
* ESOP dilution
* Promoter allotments

Red flags:

* Frequent dilution
* Favorable pricing for promoters/related parties
* Dilution without credible productive use of capital
* Persistent shareholder dilution despite weak returns

## 8. Contingent Liabilities

Check:

* Guarantees
* Legal disputes
* Tax disputes
* Regulatory disputes
* Guarantees to group companies
* Off-balance-sheet commitments

Large contingent liabilities can materially reduce downside protection.

## 9. Group Structure

Check:

* Parent company
* Subsidiaries
* Associate companies
* Joint ventures
* Listed/unlisted group entities

Red flags:

* Opaque inter-company transactions
* Circular transactions
* Frequent related-party funding
* Cash moving between entities without clear economic rationale
* Minority shareholder value leakage

## 10. Regulatory / SEBI History

Search for:

* SEBI orders
* Show-cause notices
* Exchange notices
* Insider-trading cases
* Market-manipulation cases
* Forensic audits
* Accounting investigations

Material regulatory history should be explicitly incorporated into the management/governance score.

## 11. Management Guidance

Compare:

Guidance → Actual

over approximately 3–4 years.

Red flags:

* Repeated misses
* Repeated postponement
* Frequent changes to targets
* Aggressive promises
* Unexplained divergence between guidance and delivery

Repeated guidance misses should materially reduce Management & Guidance Credibility.

## 12. Promoter Remuneration

Check:

* Promoter salary
* Commission
* Related benefits
* Remuneration versus PAT
* Remuneration versus peers

Red flag:

Very high promoter remuneration relative to:

* Company profits
* Company size
* Peer companies

## 13. Customer Concentration

Check:

* Largest customer %
* Top 5 customers
* Government dependence
* Single-contract dependence

Red flags:

* One customer contributes an unusually large percentage of revenue
* Government contract dependency
* Political/regulatory sensitivity
* Contract renewal risk

## 14. Government Dependence

Government business is not automatically negative.

Assess:

* Contract duration
* Renewal history
* Payment cycle
* Political sensitivity
* Tender competitiveness
* Customer concentration
* Regulatory dependency

A company whose growth depends heavily on continued government support should receive an appropriate risk discount.

## 15. Capital Allocation

Check:

* Acquisitions
* Capex
* Buybacks
* Dividends
* Debt repayment
* Investments
* Related-party investments

Red flags:

* Value-destructive acquisitions
* Low-return capex
* Frequent unrelated diversification
* Excessive cash deployment into promoter-linked businesses
* Persistent dilution
* Poor incremental ROCE

## 16. How to Apply Red Flags

Do not create a giant checklist in the final answer.

Only highlight material findings.

Example:

Governance

Promoter holding stable at ~55%; no material pledge.

Receivables have risen from 58 to 91 days over three years, outpacing revenue growth.

Then explain the implication briefly.

## 17. Severity

Minor

Monitor but do not materially alter thesis.

Moderate

Reduce relevant score and mention.

Major

Materially reduce Management / Financial Quality / Risk score.

Thesis-threatening

Can justify:

* Speculative Multi-Bagger
* Limited Asymmetry
* Avoid

depending on severity.

## 18. Core Principle

A high-growth story cannot compensate indefinitely for:

* Poor governance
* Weak cash generation
* Excessive leverage
* Promoter pledge
* Accounting concerns
* Value-destructive capital allocation

The goal is not to find reasons to reject companies.

The goal is to identify risks capable of destroying the multi-bagger thesis.
